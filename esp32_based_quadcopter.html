ESP32 Based Flight Controller

The story of our attempt to build an ESP32-based quadcopter for our school science expo.

Our school announced the Ibn Al Haythem Science Mela, and every student had to make a project. I was leading a small team, and our Physics Professor gave us a few ideas to choose from. One of them was a quadcopter.

I picked it because it sounded fun. I wanted to build something that mixed electronics with programming, and making our own flight controller seemed like a good challenge.

⌁ Getting started

These were the main parts we were given:

  • ESP32
  • MPU6050 sensors
  • DRV8833 motor drivers
  • LEDs
  • Coreless motors and propellers
  • 3.7V 450mAh battery

I took care of the coding, circuit connections and firmware. The rest of the team worked on the frame, body and wiring. We split the work and got started.

⌖ Where it went wrong

After almost a week, the controller was responding to the gyroscope. That part was working. The problem showed up when we increased the throttle past around 60%. The voltage dropped, and the whole system restarted.

We thought a few things might be causing it:

  • Too much weight
  • The battery not supplying enough current
  • An issue somewhere in the circuit or connections

We had just one day left before the expo. We could not find a reliable fix in time, so we decided to pause the quadcopter and prepare another project.

↳ A change of plan

That night, I worked on a smart home security system. The idea was to let it connect and sync with other smart home devices. Think of it as a Siri for the house.

We took that project to the event instead. The quadcopter never got to fly, but we did have a working demonstration of its controller.

✳ What I would do differently

Looking back, the quadcopter did not have to fly for us to show what we had built. We could have demonstrated the controller, the sensor readings and how the software responded to the gyroscope.

We could also have explained how the ESP32 and sensors would help keep the quadcopter balanced in the air. That would have shown the main idea without needing a successful takeoff.

The project did not go as planned, but the experience was worth it. It showed me how one small power issue can affect an entire system, even when other parts are working.
