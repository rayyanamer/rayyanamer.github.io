The Story So Far

■ ORIGIN & HISTORY

I'm Rayyan Amer, born in Pakistan in 2009. I'm currently an FSc Pre-Engineering student, and most of my interests somehow revolve around computers, technology, building things, and figuring out how stuff works. I didn't really plan to become a tech person. It just happened over time, starting with one old computer and a lot of curiosity.

I spent the first six years of my childhood in Phalia, a town in Mandi Bahauddin District, because my father used to work there. He had a PC at home where he played Snooker and 8 Ball Pool. Those were his favourite games. That same PC was also used for school work because my mom later became the Principal of Noor Jahan Model School during the downfall of Colony Sugar Mills, where my father worked.

And honestly, that made me feel like a celebrity at school. I'd casually tell people, "My mom is the principal of the school you study in." SHOWING DOMINANCE Every result, notice, and notification was made and printed on that same PC. I used to watch and explore all of it, and eventually I started messing around with the computer myself. I didn't know much, obviously, but I was interested in everything I could click, open, or figure out.

I also had a friend in Phalia named Daniyal. I haven't seen him in a long time, but I hope he's doing well. He was one of my best buddies back then. Those were simpler days, and I still remember that part of my childhood pretty fondly.

Then Colony Sugar Mills went through its downfall. The industry was struggling, people started moving away, and the situation became difficult for a lot of families. My family eventually moved from Phalia to our native hometown, Gojra, in late 2015. The financial situation wasn't great either. Stuck salaries and other issues put my parents under a lot of pressure, so it was a pretty tough time for us.

In 2016, my father admitted me to The Smart School Gojra after considering a number of schools. It had good infrastructure and a decent education system, so that was the choice. One thing I still find interesting is that both my maternal and paternal grandparents were from Gojra, but I had grown up in Phalia. So even though Gojra was technically my family's hometown, it didn't really feel like home to me at first.

All my friends and familiar places were back in Phalia. The people, environment, and overall vibe in Gojra felt different, and adjusting took a while. I was also pretty introverted and had social anxiety, so I usually stayed inside instead of going out and meeting people. Luckily, there was a computer at home. You can probably guess where most of my free time went.

Around 2017, I started experimenting with electrical circuits and components. One of my first projects was a small electric fan that worked by plugging it into a phone through OTG. Eid was near, so on Eid day I showed it to almost every guest who came over. They appreciated it, and I was honestly so hyped by their reactions that I wanted to build even more things. I kept experimenting with electrical components and circuits after that. Some attempts worked, some didn't, and a few taught me that electricity deserves a bit more respect than I was giving it at the time.

At school, teachers used to tell my mom that I barely spoke. Which was true. I was never really the type to talk to everyone. I'd rather have four or five people I actually knew and felt comfortable around than be surrounded by a huge group. For almost seven years, I didn't have what I'd call proper friends at school.

That changed a little when I was moved to the girls' section along with seven other boys because of CLASS STRENGTH MANAGEMENT. The boys were fewer there, and somehow I felt more comfortable. That's where I met people like Hashir, Abdullah, Ahmad, and others. After that, I gradually started having those little hello-hi conversations with more people in almost every class. Small progress, but progress.

When I was in 7th grade, my dad also taught me how to drive a car. He used to travel to Jhang Sugar Mills for work, and taking my mom and siblings to school by rickshaw was becoming difficult and expensive. So, at around 13 or 14, I learned to drive and started taking them to school and bringing them back home. The funny thing is that I'm still driving that same car. SAME CAR, YEARS LATER

At the end of 5th grade, I had also started a YouTube channel called Frictup. I uploaded Minecraft skits and Shorts, and somehow it reached almost 420 subscribers in a pretty short time. I eventually left it because of academic exams and other things. The subscribers are still there, though. I also deleted some videos that were actually pretty good because of personal disputes. Looking back, that probably wasn't my best decision.

Around 6th or 7th grade, my computer journey really picked up. I started learning about hardware, software, Windows tricks, and all sorts of things that felt ridiculously advanced to me back then. Most of what I learned came from random Indian tech channels on YouTube. Honestly, those channels deserve some credit.

Somewhere around that time, I discovered Termux and tried Nmap. It was the first tool I ever used, and also pretty much the only one for a while. Still, telling a few classmates about the things I was learning somehow gave me a reputation. The word spread that Rayyan is a Hacker. I don't know how many people actually believed that, but apparently the label stuck. If I had to describe my place in class, I'd probably say I'm a side character with a surprisingly important role. Don't ask me to explain that one.

I also started exploring Linux and different operating systems. I tried Kali Linux and Tails OS, mostly because I was curious about what they were, what made them different, and what I could learn from using them. It opened up another side of computing for me: terminals, privacy, security tools, distributions, and the idea that an operating system could be changed and customised to suit the way I wanted to use a computer. I was still learning the basics, but experimenting was half the fun.

Then came 2020. I was in 4th grade when COVID happened, and after that, the world went through a pretty drastic change. School, routines, meeting people, and everyday life all became different. It felt like someone had changed the settings of the entire world without asking us first.

And that's where the BORING ARC started. A lot of that period felt repetitive. There wasn't always much to do, days blended into each other, and life became a cycle of staying home, studying, and spending time on the computer. There were interesting little moments here and there, but overall it was a pretty quiet chapter. Also, one random fact: I've never been on a school trip. Not once. I still find that weird.

At the end of 5th grade, Frictup was already becoming a thing of the past, and as I moved into the higher classes, I kept getting more interested in computers. By 9th grade, I decided to buy a Chromebook for around PKR 6,500. That's ridiculously affordable for a working laptop, even if it was a pretty basic one.

ChromeOS was quite limited for what I wanted to do, so I started looking for a way around that. I came across MrChromebox firmware, used it to move away from the stock setup, and installed Windows on the Chromebook. I went with Tiny11 to keep things lightweight. But then I ran into the obvious problem: the Chromebook had only 16GB of storage. Not exactly spacious.

My brother and I eventually decided to install Debian instead. And it worked! That little Chromebook turned into a working LinuxBook, and somehow it still works even now. It wasn't powerful, but it was useful, and getting it running the way I wanted felt like a win.

Around that time, my dad bought a new Lenovo Yoga laptop with much better specifications, and his old HP ProBook 6460b came to us. It had Windows 10 installed, but there was a graphical artifact issue, probably related to outdated drivers. We installed Debian on it, and the issue was gone. The laptop wasn't powerful by any means, but it worked perfectly well for what we needed. Better than nothing, right?

When I look back, these old machines are a pretty big part of why I enjoy computers. I didn't always have the newest hardware, so I learned to experiment with what I had. Installing operating systems, trying different distributions, fixing problems, and making older laptops useful was genuinely fun. I wasn't just using computers anymore; I was learning how to make them work for me.

When I was in 9th grade, one of my biggest school achievements was participating in the Smart STEM Expo. Under our teacher's supervision, we built a model of the Human Nervous System, and it won first place. It was a great event, and I still remember how good it felt to see our work get recognised.

Then came the board exams. The exams themselves went pretty well, but my 9th-grade result didn't. I usually tried to keep my percentage around 90% or above, so seeing 86% on the result was a shock. It might not sound bad to everyone, but I was disappointed because I knew what I had expected from myself. I felt pretty helpless and aimless for a while, and it was a difficult period for my family too.

In 10th grade, I tried to get back on track academically while still making time for my hobbies and projects. Before the board exams, our school arranged another event called the Ibn al-Haytham Science Mela. I decided to build a drone for it, but that project didn't go as planned. You can read the story here!

Thankfully, I managed to make up for my 9th-grade result by scoring 94% in 10th grade. That brought my overall Matric percentage to around 90%. COMEBACK ARC Not bad. We take those.

And now I'm studying FSc Pre-Engineering at SAISC Gojra (Superior Allama Iqbal Science College). I've met quite a few people here, but one of the most prominent people I've met so far is Saad. It's a new chapter, with new people, a different routine, and a lot more to figure out.

From spending time around my dad's old PC in Phalia to experimenting with Linux on second-hand laptops, building little circuits, running a YouTube channel, and taking part in science exhibitions, my journey has been a mix of random interests, failed attempts, small wins, and some pretty quiet periods too.

I don't have everything figured out. I'm still learning, still experimenting, and still trying to find out what I actually want to build and where I want to go. Let's see where this script of life takes me.

See you. :3
